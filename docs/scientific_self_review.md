# Final scientific self-review

The central question is whether an occupied, conducting sector can be omitted from a specified Hall functional at a stated tolerance. A vanishing passive Hall weight does not remove its source or collision block. Exact elimination changes both the active operator and drive.

The hierarchy is physically defined before projecting any response. Active current, orthogonal active valley displacement, passive current and passive population have distinct conditional roles. M3 is already exact in the specified contact/massless driven problem; finite range can require the fourth mode. No monotonic Hall convergence or optimality among all three-dimensional spaces is claimed.

Contact invariance has an analytic Bloch-moment derivation and numerical residual checks. Finite-range rho is reported as a genuine departure from closure. Drive miss and inverse/dual sensitivity appear explicitly in the error identity; small rho alone proves no Hall bound. The difficult examples use signed modal compensation and small complementary readout sensitivity, not a universal fast-mode explanation.

Scalar/shape pieces depend on the contour projection. Fixed-budget cancellation dependence means loss of safe omission when favorable signs are removed at fixed magnitudes; it does not mean all physical perturbations preserve the budget. Main fractions exclude eta=0. Direct passive response at finite mass is separated using the uncoupled massive baseline; the large weak-case mass effect is predominantly direct readout.

Schur/Kron reduction, multirate kinetics, scattering-channel subtraction and adjoint identities are acknowledged as established. The exact-adjoint/singular certificates are conservative supporting diagnostics, not demonstrated accelerators. Lihm–Park and the other cited primary literature are positioned explicitly in the Introduction and Discussion.

The phenomenological zero-temperature, homogeneous, reversible-Born model retains only the anomalous-velocity channel. It gives no quantitative SnTe prediction, complete experimental nonlinear Hall response, general disorder independence or universal omission threshold.
